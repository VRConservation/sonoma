# Sonoma Wood Aggregation
Sonoma County contains about 513,000 acres of forest land, but under current practices, only about 3,000 acres are treated each year, leaving a treatment gap of nearly 100% (99.7%) compared with the approximately 246,000 acres believed to be treatable. These treatable acres are spread across about 14,000 private parcels—about 93% of which are less than 100 acres—so fragmentation makes individual permitting too costly and is the main reason the initiative seeks to centralize feedstock aggregation. When the land that is feasible to treat is matched with census tracts that have a poverty rate above 40%, this results in about 3,100 priority acres that are concentrated in the fire-prone northwest part of the county, which is in line with the project's Phase I aim of treating 3,000 to 4,000 acres per year and focuses risk reduction on the communities that have suffered the most from fire and at the same time have the least economic opportunity. The project suggests the establishment of a wood recovery and utilization campus at a former sawmill site within a 60-minute drive of the majority of existing timber harvesting areas and the county's most dense forest biomass, with the material being diverted into wood products, firewood, and biomass energy rather than being left as waste or burned in piles. Expected benefits include 36 direct full-time-equivalent jobs in Phase 1, increasing to 107 by 2030, plus 69 to 175 indirect jobs in forestry, prescribed fire, and support services—for a total of 85 to 191 jobs when a wider sector-wide employment multiplier is applied.
